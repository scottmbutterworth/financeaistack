Corporations Act 2001, section 254T, as read in the compilation text opened from legislation.gov.au (the 5 December 2025 text and the later compilations that carry the same heading). Open the current section before the board paper is finished: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest

Section 254T(1) says a company must not pay a dividend unless three tests are met. In the words read:

- the company's assets exceed its liabilities immediately before the dividend is declared, and the excess is sufficient for the payment of the dividend;
- the payment is fair and reasonable to the company's shareholders as a whole;
- the payment does not materially prejudice the company's ability to pay its creditors.

The note to the section gives insolvency as an example of material prejudice, and points to section 588G on insolvent trading. Section 254T(2) says assets and liabilities are calculated under the accounting standards in force at the time.

## On-market buy-back

Corporations Act section 257B, as read in the compilation at https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/2025-12-05/2025-12-05/text (re-open the current compilation):

- Section 257A: a company may buy back its shares if the buy-back does not materially prejudice its ability to pay its creditors and it follows the procedures in that Division.
- Section 257B(4): the 10/12 limit is 10 per cent of the smallest number, at any time during the last 12 months, of votes attaching to voting shares of the company.
- Section 257B(5): the proposal exceeds that limit if the votes attached to voting shares bought back in the last 12 months, plus the votes that will be bought back, exceed the 10/12 limit.
- Section 257B(6): an on-market buy-back is one that results from an offer made by a listed corporation on a declared financial market in the ordinary course of trading on that market.
- Section 257C is the shareholder-approval path if the 10/12 limit is exceeded. The procedure table in section 257B(1) is the checklist of which steps apply. Do not tick a step you have not read.

ASX. Listing Rule 3.8A is the buy-back notice rule. Appendix 3C is the form. The PDF cover dated 1 December 2019 says "Rule 3.8A" and says an Appendix 3C is the form ASX makes available on ASX Online from time to time: https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/rules-guidance-notes-waivers/asx-listing-rules/appendices/Appendix_03C.pdf

Listed@ASX Compliance Update 05/21 says that, for a buy-back announced after the 5 June 2021 change, the entity uses the online Appendix 3C for the announcement, for a change, for the daily notice (Part 4), and for the final notice (Part 5). It does not keep using the old paper Appendices 3D, 3E, and 3F. https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/documents/listings/supervision-updates/2021/listed-at-compliance-update-june-2021.pdf

The daily section of the online form, as it appears on announcements lodged in 2026, says the daily notice is due at least half an hour before trading starts on the business day after a day on which securities are bought back, and it cites Listing Rule 3.8A. An older Chapter 3 file at https://www.asx.com.au/documents/rules/Chapter03.pdf still describes Appendices 3D, 3E, and 3F, and it says the on-market Appendix 3C is given immediately the company decides that it wants to buy back shares. Do not follow the 3D/3E/3F table for a buy-back announced after 5 June 2021. Open the current Chapter 3 and the current online form and use the timing printed there.
